Godrays 1 — occluded radial blur

Crepuscular rays / "godrays" as a post-process buildFilterShader. For each output pixel, the shader walks in screen-space toward a fixed sun UV, sampling the already-rendered canvas at each step and adding a decaying weighted sum back on top of the base pixel.

The sum is high when the line from pixel to sun passes through the bright disc, and low when it's interrupted by dark foreground geometry — so the bright streaks automatically occlude against silhouettes. Move the cube/sphere/torus in front of the sun and you'll see the rays cut behind them.

Knobs in prepGodraysFilter:

  • samples — number of steps along the ray (32 here). More = smoother and heavier.
  • density — how far each pixel reaches toward the light (0..1).
  • decay — per-step fadeoff. <1 weights closer-to-source samples higher.
  • exposure — overall strength of the added rays.

The scene is set up specifically to suit this filter: a near-white sun against a very dark sky, with near-black foreground shapes. Higher contrast = more visible rays. Compare with godrays 2 for a cheaper version that ignores geometry.
